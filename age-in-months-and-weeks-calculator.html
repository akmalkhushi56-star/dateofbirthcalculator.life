Why age in months or weeks matters

Years are the default unit, but they are not always the most useful one. New parents often track a baby's age in weeks and months for the first year or two, since development milestones are usually described that way. Later in life, months and weeks are less common, but still handy for precise comparisons or for satisfying pure curiosity.

This calculator gives you all four units side by side: years, total months, total weeks, and total days, all calculated from the same exact date so they stay consistent with each other.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert my age from years to months?

Enter your date of birth and the calculator returns your exact age in total months, calculated from full calendar months rather than a flat multiplication by twelve.

Why is age in months not just years times twelve?

Because months are not all the same length, and the current partial month needs to be counted correctly. This tool counts actual calendar months between your birth date and today.

Is this useful for tracking a baby's age?

Yes, many parents use week and month based age tracking for infants, since growth and development milestones are usually described in those units.
